## What version one actually does

1. Maps 6,361 parcels, 5,080 buildings and 9,759 entrances. The rectangle includes part of Campidoglio and is not an official San Donato neighbourhood boundary.
2. Uses official ISTAT 2021 section polygons and Torino 2023 counts. Spatial allocation estimates dwellings and households in 3,731 residential buildings. It withholds allocation for 395 residential buildings in partial boundary sections. There are 954 non-residential buildings.
3. Provides parcel, building, entrance and inspected APE attribute tables, search, numeric filtering, sorting, pagination and full-precision CSV export.
4. Shows two individually inspected public APE records for Via Cordero di Pamparato 17, parcel 1175/434. One residential property references subalterno 75. One non-residential property references subalterni 8 and 9 in a single APE. Both PDF forms mark `Unità immobiliare`; do not turn the second record into two apartments or duplicate its energy totals.
5. Uses ARERA 2025 Piemonte resident domestic supplies (>1.5–3 kW, all markets) as a statistical electricity benchmark. Twelve monthly means reconcile to the published annual value of 1,609.908 kWh. It is not a measured apartment bill or a fixed-cohort annual total.
6. Uses four JRC PVGIS 5.3 SARAH3 scenarios at one representative location, with 1 kWp and 14% system losses. South/east/west 30° and horizontal 0° yield 1,338.88 / 1,044.27 / 1,043.65 / 1,097.53 kWh/kWp/year. Actual roof planes and urban shading are unresolved.
7. Provides optional explicit unit/property PV shares, initially unassigned, stored only in browser local storage. Shares are scenario assumptions, not ownership, installed generation or measured shared energy.
8. Adds four GSE 2025–2027 conventional primary-substation areas intersecting the rectangle. Coordinate membership is indicative; an official POD lookup takes precedence near boundaries.
9. Renders a LoD1-style building scene from existing technical-map height attributes. 4,977 heights are present; 103 use an explicit 3 m/floor fallback. It has schematic flat roofs and no apartment interiors.
10. Downloads an enhanced GIS package with six GeoPackage layers (EPSG:32632), six CSV tables and source notes. APE points are explicitly entrance proxies, not apartment geometry.

## Critical limits and mistakes to avoid

- Individual apartment coverage is two inspected certificates at one address, not the whole district. The SIPEE portal returned 15 records there, including cancelled records; only two PDFs were inspected.
- APE count is not a confirmed minimum apartment count. Renewals, cancellations and multiple cadastral identifiers matter.
- The inspected APE GIS coordinates (45.116667, 7.716667) are far from the actual parcel and were rejected. Use cadastral identifiers and address evidence; no exact interior position is known.
- APE primary-energy intensity, net heating need, standard-use electricity, district heat and measured electrical consumption are different quantities. Preserve their units and source semantics.
- The two PDFs declare different building-unit totals (35 and 1) and construction years that differ from inherited map attributes. No authoritative building total or year was inferred.
- Census estimates are continuous allocations; 22.5 estimated dwellings does not mean 22.5 identified apartments. Never generate fake subalterni, PODs or apartment polygons from these counts.
- Legacy `SEZCENS` values were incompatible with newer geography. Do not join census vintages using a bare section number. The current integration uses official polygons and representative-point spatial joins.
- The ARERA provincial 2023 archive encountered in research contains BTA/ATECO non-domestic rows. It was excluded from the residential benchmark.
- Annual PV/demand ratio is not self-consumption, self-sufficiency or CER shared energy. Incomplete-demand scopes suppress the ratio in the UI/export.
- The existing 3D scene is not a surveyed LoD2/2.5 dataset or a validated CityGML model. ARPA 3D 2017 metadata was verified but its data was not imported. It documents Torino eaves heights from the 2015 technical map, not roof-plane geometry.
- Piemonte DSM ICE 2009–2011 metadata describes a 5 m grid. Raster data was not imported; do not claim dormer-scale roof analysis from it.
- The newly indexed municipal DBGT page could not be opened during research. Actual downloadable LoD2/2.5 coverage remains unverified.
- Company data/PODs must go into explicitly approved private storage, never the public static site or public Git history.
